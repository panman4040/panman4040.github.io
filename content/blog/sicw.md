Wah, this is my first CTF as well as my first time getting into the final of such a big event. Got some seniors carrying the qualifier but sadly couldn't participate at the last minute, so we were from 4 down to 2 (oof).

We got 18th out of that tho, not bottom place so that's a plus (maybe?). But I did learn a lot about events like this, as well as meeting up with awesome people from NTU Sentinels. It was good vibes all the way yay

![](/images/77d54c7e-a6ea-442f-8a5f-064320967237.jpg)

But I feel like one thing about CTFs is that the spirit is kinda gone now. I mean the whole thing is just who clanks better than who in my opinion. The challenges themselves were quite well made, and the organisers' energy was another level. I can feel their passion, tinkering stuff to work on the best final they possibly could. But what's the point if nobody is reading the problem statement anyway, just `Ctrl + C` `Ctrl + V` to AI.

Now I'm not saying that "AI bad huh huh", it's actually a great way to learn new stuff easily without being gatekept by people like ye olden days. But I think that for beginners like me, we should treat the AI as supplementary, use it to learn, rather than being performative and clank to top 1 or top 2 without knowing anything. 

I think it's also inevitable that AI will come into play for competitions such as this, and I don't blame people for using it. I mean if we got diesel cars, why bother going back to steam engines? But I think there should be a balance, one for understanding the underlying concepts, and another for optimizing strategies using agents. Knowing both will serve a powerful skillset that the average clankers don't have, especially in this day and age (in my opinion).

Anyway I got a sick badge check it out!!11!!!:
![](/images/a340b49f-adb0-4934-b7cb-0c73a3e4cb32.jpg)

I also did attend the GovWare conference, went around some booths, collected free stuffs. Literally all the booths sell some variations of agentic SOC, or agentic IR tools. That goes to show how AI permeates everything now, even senior-levelled disciplines like cybersecurity. And not adapting to the new fad only holds you back. I think it's time for me to put down my stupid ego and embrace the new trend

![](/images/688489a8-b618-475f-963a-d0ab01e10419.jpg)

Anyway, it was a good time. Going out to events of this scale really opens up my horizon (god that sounds so cheesy), and I hope I'm able to go on more conferences like this some time
